# Jev atomic questions versus global Choice

The user highlighted Jev's parallel question evaluation and non-deterministic responses. The [official primitives guide](https://docs.typesafe.ai/primitives) says questions sharing one structured state are evaluated independently and in parallel. We froze a second synthetic fixture before dispatch: ten more queries in Italian, Dutch, Polish, Catalan, and Japanese over 20 new fictional enterprise passages. Together with the earlier English/Spanish/French/Portuguese/German fixture, the probe has 20 queries over ten languages. Translation families share facts, so 20 is not 20 independent domains or a held-out sample.

Three approaches saw the same four candidates per query: the existing Noul per passage, four path-specific atomic Noul questions in one request, and a single Choice among four passages plus `none`. The pinned local multilingual TEI reranker was the control. Eight of ten new cases received fresh per-passage Noul assessments (32 calls); all 20 cases received packed Noul and Choice (44 calls including four predeclared exact repeats). The prior ten-case per-passage Noul and TEI results were reused without another call. The first English packed request timed out at the 20-second deadline; its companion Choice request failed validation with a `ValueError`. No raw non-Score body was stored, so the exact Choice defect is unresolved. Both failures count against operational completion. Two Italian/Japanese cases were repeated once for each bundled method; all four repeated value vectors matched. Four identical observations are insufficient to establish determinism, and other inputs may vary. There was no ranking gain over TEI on these easy self-authored labels. Choice is not promoted, and the atomic bundle remains a research option pending human-labeled difficult cases, source-scope checks, full-order contract tests, load, and a larger variance sample.
